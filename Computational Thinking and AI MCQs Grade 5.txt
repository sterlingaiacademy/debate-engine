Computational Thinking & AI Benchmark Test (Grade 5)
Instructions: Read each scenario carefully and choose the correct answer (A, B, C, or D). Answers and detailed explanations are provided after each question.
Section 1: Computational Thinking
Question 1
Scenario: You are tasked with organizing a massive school library with thousands of unorganized books. Instead of tackling the entire library at once, you break the task into smaller steps: sorting books by genre, then by author’s last name, and finally by publication year.
Which Computational Thinking concept are you using when you break this large task into smaller, manageable sub-tasks?
A) Abstraction
B) Decomposition
C) Algorithm Design
D) Pattern Recognition
Correct Answer: B) Decomposition
Explanation: Decomposition involves breaking down a complex problem or system into smaller, more manageable parts. By dividing the library organization into distinct sorting steps (genre, author, publication year), the overall task becomes easier to solve.
Question 2
Scenario: Maya notices that every time she enters a password on different school websites, the system checks for three things: at least one capital letter, at least one number, and at least 8 characters long.
What Computational Thinking skill is Maya using by identifying these common rules across different websites?
A) Pattern Recognition
B) Debugging
C) Evaluation
D) Data Collection
Correct Answer: A) Pattern Recognition
Explanation: Pattern recognition involves looking for similarities, trends, or repeating rules among different problems. Maya noticed a repeating set of password requirements across multiple sites.
Question 3
Scenario: A student is creating a digital map of the school playground for new students. The map shows the positions of the swings, slides, and soccer field, but leaves out individual blades of grass, tiny pebbles, and ant hills.
Why did the student leave out those tiny details?
A) The student did not have enough time to draw them.
B) The student used abstraction to focus only on important information needed to navigate the playground.
C) The map algorithm stopped working properly.
D) The tiny details are part of a hidden pattern.
Correct Answer: B) The student used abstraction to focus only on important information needed to navigate the playground.
Explanation: Abstraction means removing unnecessary details so you can focus on the essential features needed to solve a problem or represent an idea clearly.
Question 4
Scenario: Consider the following recipe to make a simple fruit smoothie:
	•	Peel 1 banana and slice it.
	•	Place sliced banana and 5 strawberries into a blender.
	•	Add 1 cup of milk into the blender.
	•	Put the lid securely on the blender.
	•	Blend on high speed for 30 seconds.
What is this step-by-step set of instructions called in computer science?
A) A Variable
B) An Algorithm
C) A Loop
D) A Database
Correct Answer: B) An Algorithm
Explanation: An algorithm is a sequence of precise, step-by-step instructions designed to accomplish a specific task or solve a particular problem.
Question 5
Scenario: A cleaning robot is programmed to navigate a grid room. It follows this sequence of commands: Move Forward 2 units -> Turn Right 90° -> Move Forward 3 units.
If the robot starts at position (0, 0) facing North, what is its final position and direction?
A) Position (2, 3), facing East
B) Position (3, 2), facing North
C) Position (3, 2), facing East
D) Position (2, 3), facing South
Correct Answer: C) Position (3, 2), facing East
Explanation: Starting at (0, 0) facing North: moving forward 2 units takes it to (0, 2). Turning right 90° makes it face East. Moving forward 3 units East changes its X-coordinate by +3, landing at (3, 2) facing East.
Question 6
Scenario: Look at the following sequence of numbers: 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, ___
What is the next number in this sequence, and what rule generates it?
A) 50; Add 2 to the previous number.
B) 96; Multiply the previous number by 2.
C) 72; Add 24 to the previous number.
D) 60; Multiply the previous number by 3.
Correct Answer: B) 96; Multiply the previous number by 2.
Explanation: Each number in the sequence is double the previous number (3×2=6, 6×2=12, 12×2=24, 24×2=48, 48×2=96). This relies on pattern recognition.
Question 7
Scenario: An automatic sprinkler system is programmed with the following rule:
IF (Soil is Dry) AND (Time is Evening) THEN Turn On Sprinkler
Under which of the following conditions will the sprinkler turn ON?
A) Soil is Dry and Time is Morning.
B) Soil is Wet and Time is Evening.
C) Soil is Dry and Time is Evening.
D) Soil is Wet and Time is Morning.
Correct Answer: C) Soil is Dry and Time is Evening.
Explanation: The logical operator AND requires BOTH conditions to be true simultaneously. The sprinkler only turns on when the soil is dry AND the time is evening.

Question 9

How many times will the word "Hello" be printed?

B) 3 times

Question 10
Scenario: A student wants to draw a square using a program. A square has 4 equal sides and 4 right-angle (90°) turns. They write this code:
REPEAT 4 TIMES:     Move Forward 100 steps     Turn Right 45 degrees END REPEAT
The output shape is an octagon fragment instead of a square. How should the code be corrected?
A) Change "REPEAT 4 TIMES" to "REPEAT 8 TIMES".
B) Change "Move Forward 100 steps" to "Move Forward 50 steps".
C) Change "Turn Right 45 degrees" to "Turn Right 90 degrees".
D) Change "REPEAT 4 TIMES" to "REPEAT 2 TIMES".
Correct Answer: C) Change "Turn Right 45 degrees" to "Turn Right 90 degrees".
Explanation: Internal/external turns for a square require 90-degree rotations. Turning 45 degrees causes incorrect geometry, so updating the angle to 90 degrees fixes the bug.
Question 11
Scenario: Two algorithms are tested to sort a list of 10 student names alphabetically:
• Algorithm A takes 45 seconds to sort the list.
• Algorithm B takes 3 seconds to sort the same list and uses less computer memory.
Which statement best evaluates these two algorithms?
A) Algorithm A is better because it takes more time to process thoroughly.
B) Algorithm B is more efficient because it solves the same problem in less time and uses fewer resources.
C) Both algorithms are equally good because they give the same result.
D) Neither algorithm works because algorithms cannot sort names.
Correct Answer: B) Algorithm B is more efficient because it solves the same problem in less time and uses fewer resources.

Question 13
Scenario: You are designing a video game character that can jump. You write a single function block called Jump() containing 5 lines of movement instructions. Every time the player presses the Spacebar, the game simply runs Jump().
Which computational concept makes your code cleaner by grouping instructions into a reusable block?
A) Modularity / Functions
B) Data Mining
C) Sensor Calibration

Section 2: Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning
Question 14
Scenario: An AI system is being trained to recognize photos of apples. Scientists feed the system 5,000 photos labeled "Apple" and 5,000 photos labeled "Not Apple". The system learns to identify common features like roundness, stems, and color patterns.
What type of AI learning process is described here?
A) Unsupervised Learning
B) Supervised Learning
C) Manual Programming
D) Hardware Processing
Correct Answer: B) Supervised Learning
Explanation: Supervised learning is a machine learning technique where an AI model is trained using labeled datasets (inputs paired with the correct output labels like "Apple" / "Not Apple").
Question 15
Scenario: A school cafeteria uses an automated facial recognition camera to let students pay for lunch without cards. However, the system frequently fails to recognize students with darker skin tones or students wearing glasses.
What is the most likely cause of this issue?
A) The camera runs out of electricity.
B) The training data used to train the AI contained mostly photos of people with lighter skin and without glasses (Biased Data).
C) Facial recognition systems only work on adults.
D) The computer algorithm is too fast.
Correct Answer: B) The training data used to train the AI contained mostly photos of people with lighter skin and without glasses (Biased Data).
Explanation: AI models learn directly from training data. If the dataset lacks diversity, the AI becomes biased and performs poorly on unrepresented groups.
Question 16

A) Traditional programming requires electricity, while Machine Learning does not.
B) In traditional programming, humans write explicit rules; in Machine Learning, the computer discovers rules by analyzing data.
C) Traditional programs can only process numbers, while AI can only process text.
D) Machine Learning never makes errors, whereas traditional code always has bugs.
Correct Answer: B) In traditional programming, humans write explicit rules; in Machine Learning, the computer discovers rules by analyzing data.
