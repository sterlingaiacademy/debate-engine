Grade 7 Social Science: Benchmark Test Assessment (PISA/IBT Style)
Curriculum Alignment: NCERT Class 7 Social Science (Our Pasts - II, Our Environment, Social and Political Life - II)
Assessment Type: Competency and Application-Based Multiple Choice Questions
Section 1: Our Environment & Earth Movements (Geography)

1. Earthquakes release seismic waves from an underground focus point. Which location experiences the most severe surface damage during an earthquake?
A. The focus point deep inside the Earth's mantle
B. The epicenter located directly above the focus on the Earth's surface
C. Coastal plains located 500 km away from the fault line
D. High-altitude mountain plateaus in the opposite hemisphere
Correct Answer: B. The epicenter located directly above the focus on the Earth's surface
Explanation: Seismic energy travels outwards in all directions, but the surface point closest to the focus—the epicenter—receives the highest concentration of shockwave energy first, suffering maximum structural damage.

2. A student collects two rock samples: Sample A has large, visible mineral crystals formed underground over thousands of years, while Sample B has smooth, tiny glass-like grain structures formed rapidly on the surface. How are Sample A and Sample B classified?
A. Sample A is Extrusive Igneous; Sample B is Intrusive Igneous.
B. Sample A is Intrusive Igneous; Sample B is Extrusive Igneous.
C. Sample A is Sedimentary; Sample B is Metamorphic.
D. Sample A is Metamorphic; Sample B is Sedimentary.
Correct Answer: B. Sample A is Intrusive Igneous; Sample B is Extrusive Igneous.
Explanation: Magma cooling slowly beneath the Earth's surface forms coarse-grained intrusive igneous rocks (granite). Molten lava cooling rapidly on the exterior surface forms fine-grained extrusive igneous rocks (basalt).

3. Why does the Troposphere contain nearly all of Earth's atmospheric weather phenomena (like clouds, rain, and fog) compared to higher atmospheric layers?
A. It has the highest concentration of ozone gas.
B. It contains almost all of the atmosphere's water vapor and atmospheric density.
C. It is completely shielded from solar ultraviolet radiation.
D. It experiences constant zero-gravity conditions.
Correct Answer: B. It contains almost all of the atmosphere's water vapor and atmospheric density.
Explanation: Gravity pulls ~80% of total atmospheric mass and almost all water vapor into the troposphere (the lowest layer), providing the necessary moisture and density for cloud formation and weather.

4. In desert landforms, mushroom rocks with narrow bases and wider tops are frequently observed. What geological process shapes these landforms?
A. Glacial melting eroding the bottom rock strata
B. Wind carrying abrasive sand particles eroding the lower portion of rocks faster than the upper part
C. Underground water dissolving calcium carbonate at ground level
D. Volcanic ash deposits forming soft bases
Correct Answer: B. Wind carrying abrasive sand particles eroding the lower portion of rocks faster than the upper part
Explanation: Wind-blown sand stays relatively close to the ground (due to gravity). As wind sweeps past rock formations, the lower portion undergoes higher friction and erosion, carving out a mushroom shape.
Section 2: Water & Global Ecosystems (Geography)

5. Tides in oceans are periodic rising and falling of ocean water occurring twice a day. What causes these tides?
A. Underwater volcanic eruptions on ocean floors
B. Gravitational pull exerted by the Moon and the Sun on Earth's surface
C. Seasonal shifts in global trade winds
D. Variations in ocean water temperature near equator
Correct Answer: B. Gravitational pull exerted by the Moon and the Sun on Earth's surface
Explanation: The strong gravitational forces exerted by the Moon and Sun pull on Earth’s ocean water, causing rhythmic ocean tides twice daily.

6. Tropical Rainforests (such as the Amazon Basin) remain evergreen throughout the year. Why do these forests never shed all their leaves simultaneously?
A. The trees are artificially watered by river floods.
B. There is no distinct dry season, and warm, humid rain occurs almost daily.
C. The soil contains unique anti-freezing minerals.
D. Trees shed leaves only once every ten years.
Correct Answer: B. There is no distinct dry season, and warm, humid rain occurs almost daily.
Explanation: Tropical equatorial regions receive continuous solar energy and heavy rainfall year-round. Without a dry period to conserve water, trees shed and grow leaves continuously rather than all at once.

7. Compare the coniferous forests (Taiga) with tropical deciduous forests. What adaptation allows coniferous trees to survive heavy snow and freezing conditions?
A. Large broad leaves that collect sunlight during snowstorms
B. Needle-like leaves and flexible sloped branches that shed heavy snow easily
C. Thick fleshy stems that store liquid water
D. Shallow root networks exposed above snow cover
Correct Answer: B. Needle-like leaves and flexible sloped branches that shed heavy snow easily
Explanation: Needle-shaped leaves with waxy coatings reduce water loss via transpiration, while conical branches allow heavy snow to slide off without snapping branches.
Section 3: Medieval Indian History & Empires (History)

8. During the Chola Dynasty, village assemblies called 'Sabha' managed local irrigation, land records, and taxation. How were committee members selected in these assemblies according to Chola inscriptions (e.g., Uttaramerur)?
A. Members were appointed directly by the Chola Emperor.
B. Eligible candidates' names were written on palm-leaf slips and drawn randomly by a young boy.
C. Positions were sold at public auctions to the highest taxpaying family.
D. Military officers voted on local village commanders.
Correct Answer: B. Eligible candidates' names were written on palm-leaf slips and drawn randomly by a young boy.
Explanation: The Uttaramerur inscription describes a system where eligible candidates (meeting age, property, and conduct criteria) had their names placed in an urn, and a young child drew the slips to ensure fair selection.

9. Under the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal Empire, the 'Iqta' or 'Jagir' system was used for governance. What was the primary responsibility of an Iqradar or Jagirdar?
A. Writing official state history manuscripts in Persian
B. Collecting land revenue from assigned territories to maintain troops and keep local peace
C. Building royal tombs exclusively in capital cities
D. Controlling foreign ocean maritime trade routes
Correct Answer: B. Collecting land revenue from assigned territories to maintain troops and keep local peace
Explanation: Jagirdars/Iqtadars were military commanders granted rights to collect land revenue (*jama*) from designated land grants (*jagir/iqta*) to pay their salaries and maintain military contingents for the king.

10. Raja Todar Mal, Emperor Akbar’s revenue minister, introduced the 'Zabt' system. How was agricultural tax calculated under this system?
A. A fixed 50% tax was charged on all lands regardless of crop yields.
B. Crops and fields were surveyed and measured carefully over a 10-year period to fix taxes based on actual yield and market prices.
C. Tax was collected only in the form of gold coins.
D. Farmers paid taxes only during drought years.
Correct Answer: B. Crops and fields were surveyed and measured carefully over a 10-year period to fix taxes based on actual yield and market prices.
Explanation: Todar Mal conducted a survey of crop yields, cultivated areas, and prices over a ten-year period (1570–1580), establishing a tax rate based on average productivity per crop.

11. Why did medieval rulers across India lavishly build grand temples, mosques, and monumental architecture like fortresses and tombs?
A. To comply with international trade laws
B. To project their power, wealth, divine sanction, and political legitimacy to subjects and rivals
C. To provide housing for common agricultural laborers
D. Because stone was cheaper than mud brick construction
Correct Answer: B. To project their power, wealth, divine sanction, and political legitimacy to subjects and rivals
Explanation: Monumental structures served as visible symbols of a ruler’s devotion, military strength, economic prosperity, and divine authority.
Section 4: Social Transformations, Devotion & Regional Cultures (History)

12. How did many tribal communities (such as the Ahoms of Assam or Gonds of Central India) transform as they established centralized medieval states?
A. They abandoned agriculture entirely to become nomadic hunters.
B. A social hierarchy developed, tribal chiefs merged into the ruling class, and specialized administrative systems were adopted.
C. They ceased all contact with non-tribal merchants.
D. They converted entirely into coastal fishing communities.
Correct Answer: B. A social hierarchy developed, tribal chiefs merged into the ruling class, and specialized administrative systems were adopted.
Explanation: As tribes formed kingdoms (like Garha Katanga for Gonds or the Ahom state), society split into unequal classes, chiefs gained Rajput status, and administrative systems like the *Paik* labor system were created.

13. The Bhakti and Sufi movements swept across medieval India between the 8th and 17th centuries. What was a defining characteristic shared by Bhakti saints (like Kabir and Mirabai) and Sufi mystics?
A. They conducted spiritual teachings exclusively in formal Sanskrit.
B. They rejected rigid ritualism, caste discrimination, and emphasized personal love and devotion to God.
C. They urged disciples to isolate themselves completely from community life.
D. They supported religious wars between rival kingdoms.
Correct Answer: B. They rejected rigid ritualism, caste discrimination, and emphasized personal love and devotion to God.
Explanation: Both Bhakti and Sufi reformers preached devotion (*bhakti*/*ishq*), human brotherhood, and social equality, opposing ritualistic traditions and caste divisions.

14. The growth of regional languages, such as Bengali or Malayalam, during the medieval period was heavily influenced by which development?
A. Universal colonial laws dictating language policies
B. Patronage by local rulers, translation of epics into local vernaculars, and Bhakti devotional poetry
C. Total abandonment of oral storytelling traditions
D. Standardized printing presses established by European traders
Correct Answer: B. Patronage by local rulers, translation of epics into local vernaculars, and Bhakti devotional poetry
Explanation: Local rulers (like the Cheras in Kerala or Sultans in Bengal) patronized regional court literature, while Bhakti poets composed songs in regional languages, enriching everyday vernaculars.
Section 5: Democracy, Gender & Institutional Equality (Civics)

15. In India, Universal Adult Suffrage grants every citizen who is 18 years or older the right to vote. What democratic principle does this practice uphold?
A. Economic equality regardless of tax bracket
B. Political equality—one person, one vote, one value
C. Absolute judicial freedom
D. Educational entitlement for adults
Correct Answer: B. Political equality—one person, one vote, one value
Explanation: Universal Adult Suffrage ensures political equality by giving every adult citizen one vote with equal weight, regardless of wealth, gender, caste, or education level.

16. Read the situation: A private hospital charges high fees and refuses treatment to a poor patient, while a public government hospital offers free or low-cost care but has long waiting lines. What healthcare issue in India does this highlight?
A. Public healthcare is always superior in quality to private care.
B. Inequity in healthcare access, where quality treatment often depends on an individual's financial capacity.
C. Complete absence of medical professionals in urban centers.
D. Government prohibition of private medical clinics.
Correct Answer: B. Inequity in healthcare access, where quality treatment often depends on an individual's financial capacity.
Explanation: Private healthcare offers immediate care but is expensive, whereas public healthcare is affordable but under-resourced, reflecting socio-economic inequality in essential services.

17. Historically, unpaid household chores (cooking, cleaning, caregiving) performed largely by women have been viewed as 'natural duties' rather than 'productive economic work'. How does social science categorize this issue?
A. Gender-neutral economic labor
B. Gender devaluation of caregiving and domestic work
C. Formal market-based employment
D. Voluntary charity work
Correct Answer: B. Gender devaluation of caregiving and domestic work
Explanation: Society often devalues domestic and caregiving labor done by women within families by not recognizing it as real economic work, perpetuating gender inequality.

18. How do Independent Media channels play a vital role in a functioning constitutional democracy?
A. By broadcasting only government-approved achievements
B. By holding government actions accountable, presenting balanced news, and providing a public platform for debate
C. By replacing elected parliamentarians in lawmaking
D. By controlling private corporate profits directly
Correct Answer: B. By holding government actions accountable, presenting balanced news, and providing a public platform for debate
Explanation: An independent media acts as the "fourth pillar" of democracy, providing unbiased information so citizens can make informed political choices and scrutinize government policies.
Section 6: Markets, Trade & Economic Chains (Civics)

19. Consider the 'Chain of Markets' for a cotton shirt: Seed Farmer → Trader → Wholesale Cloth Merchant → Garment Manufacturer → Supermarket Retailer → Final Consumer. Who along this chain typically receives the lowest profit margin relative to their labor?
A. The Supermarket Retailer
B. The Small Cotton Farmer
C. The Garment Manufacturer
D. The Wholesale Merchant
Correct Answer: B. The Small Cotton Farmer
Explanation: Small farmers face high input costs (fertilizers, seeds) and low bargaining power, receiving low raw cotton prices compared to big retailers who sell finished shirts at higher profit margins.

20. What advantage do weekly local markets (*Haats*) offer low-income consumers compared to air-conditioned shopping malls?
A. Weekly markets sell branded luxury goods imported from abroad.
B. Weekly markets offer cheaper unbranded goods, allow price bargaining, and feature multiple competing sellers in one spot.
C. Weekly markets provide long-term product warranties and credit cards.
D. Weekly markets are open 24 hours a day continuously.
Correct Answer: B. Weekly markets offer cheaper unbranded goods, allow price bargaining, and feature multiple competing sellers in one spot.
Explanation: Sellers in weekly markets don't pay high rent, electricity bills, or store overheads, allowing them to offer essential goods at lower, bargainable prices.

21. In a 'putting-out system' (*Dadni*) in the textile weaving sector, a merchant supplies yarn to weavers and buys back the finished cloth. What is a major disadvantage of this system for weavers?
A. Weavers are forced to buy expensive looms from foreign countries.
B. Weavers depend entirely on merchants for raw materials and market access, giving merchants power to dictate low payment prices.
C. Weavers must set up large factories in capital cities.
D. Merchants share 80% of retail store profits directly with weavers.
Correct Answer: B. Weavers depend entirely on merchants for raw materials and market access, giving merchants power to dictate low payment prices.
Explanation: Since weavers lack market contacts and raw material capital, merchants control prices, leaving weavers vulnerable to exploitation despite their weaving labor.

22. How do Advertising campaigns primarily influence consumer behavior in a competitive market economy?
A. By forcing local governments to lower import tariffs
B. By creating brand loyalty, emotional appeal, and perceived product value among target buyers
C. By making unbranded goods completely illegal
D. By fixing market prices through legal decrees
Correct Answer: B. By creating brand loyalty, emotional appeal, and perceived product value among target buyers
Explanation: Advertisements build brand image using appealing visuals, celebrity endorsements, and lifestyle claims to convince consumers that branded items are superior to unbranded alternatives.

23. In human-environment interactions, how does the vegetation of the Amazon Basin compare with the vegetation of the Ganga-Brahmaputra Basin?
A. Amazon is dominated by dense equatorial rainforests, while Ganga-Brahmaputra features tropical deciduous forests, mangroves, and extensive agricultural crops.
B. Both basins consist purely of cold tundra mosses.
C. Amazon has extensive wheat fields, while Ganga-Brahmaputra has dense pine needles.
D. Neither basin supports plant growth due to excessive salt content.
Correct Answer: A. Amazon is dominated by dense equatorial rainforests, while Ganga-Brahmaputra features tropical deciduous forests, mangroves, and extensive agricultural crops.
Explanation: The equatorial climate of the Amazon supports dense rainforests, whereas the monsoon climate of the Ganga-Brahmaputra basin supports deciduous trees, Sundarbans mangroves, and intensive agriculture (paddy, jute, tea).

24. What primary geographical factor explains why Ladakh is a cold desert while the Thar is a hot desert?
A. Ladakh is located near the ocean, while Thar is landlocked.
B. Ladakh is located at a high altitude in the rain-shadow region of the Himalayas, while Thar is located in a low-latitude tropical plain.
C. Thar receives heavy snowfall every winter.
D. Ladakh experiences intense volcanic heat from beneath.
Correct Answer: B. Ladakh is located at a high altitude in the rain-shadow region of the Himalayas, while Thar is located in a low-latitude tropical plain.
Explanation: High elevation (3,000m+) and placement in the Himalayan rain shadow give Ladakh a frigid, dry cold-desert climate, whereas Thar’s low elevation in tropical latitudes makes it a hot desert.

25. A civil society group in a city organizes a peaceful demonstration, files Right to Information (RTI) petitions, and writes newspaper articles to stop illegal tree felling in a public park. What democratic right are they exercising?
A. Right to enforce judicial imprisonment
B. Constitutional rights to freedom of expression, peaceful assembly, and public participation
C. Right to override parliamentary statutes
D. Unconstitutional resistance against local municipal laws
Correct Answer: B. Constitutional rights to freedom of expression, peaceful assembly, and public participation
Explanation: Democratic constitutions guarantee citizens freedom of speech, peaceful protest, and access to information (RTI) to hold authorities accountable and protect civic interests.
