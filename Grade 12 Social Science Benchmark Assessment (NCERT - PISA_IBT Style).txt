Grade 12 Advanced Civics & Social Theory Study Guide
25 Multiple Choice Questions: Society, Citizenship, Globalisation, and Civic Responsibilities
This document contains a set of 25 advanced practice multiple-choice questions tailored for Grade 12 students. Each question addresses higher-level concepts in political philosophy, international relations, global governance, and advanced sociological theory, accompanied by the correct answer and a comprehensive explanation.

1. According to Immanuel Wallerstein’s World Systems Theory, how is global economic inequality structured?
	•	A) Equally across all nation-states based on natural resource distribution
	•	B) Into Core, Semi-Periphery, and Periphery regions where core nations exploit peripheral labor and resources
	•	C) Exclusively along ideological divisions between capitalist and socialist states
	•	D) Dynamically through regional free trade agreements with no dominant centers
Answer: B) Into Core, Semi-Periphery, and Periphery regions where core nations exploit peripheral labor and resources
Explanation: Wallerstein's World Systems Theory divides the global economy into Core (dominant capitalist countries), Periphery (dependent, exploited nations providing raw materials and cheap labor), and Semi-Periphery (in-between buffer zones), emphasizing structural international inequality.

2. What key distinction does Karl Marx make between the 'Bourgeoisie' and the 'Proletariat' in capitalist society?
	•	A) The Bourgeoisie own the means of production, while the Proletariat sell their labor power.
	•	B) The Bourgeoisie hold political elected office, while the Proletariat hold judicial appointments.
	•	C) The Bourgeoisie favor direct democracy, while the Proletariat support monarchical rule.
	•	D) The Bourgeoisie are rural landowners, while the Proletariat live exclusively in urban communes.
Answer: A) The Bourgeoisie own the means of production, while the Proletariat sell their labor power.
Explanation: In Marxist conflict theory, society is split into two primary classes: the Bourgeoisie (the capitalist class owning factories, land, and capital) and the Proletariat (the working class forced to sell labor for wages).

3. In international relations, what defines the political theory of "Realism"?
	•	A) Nations operate primarily through international institutions and moral international law.
	•	B) The international system is anarchic, and sovereign states act self-interestedly to maximize power and security.
	•	C) Non-state actors like NGOs are the primary driving forces behind international stability.
	•	D) Global trade naturally eliminates conflict and guarantees universal peace.
Answer: B) The international system is anarchic, and sovereign states act self-interestedly to maximize power and security.
Explanation: Realism posits that international politics is fundamentally an anarchic struggle for power and security among sovereign states prioritizing national self-interest over moral ideals.

4. Which theoretical concept, formulated by Antonio Gramsci, explains how a dominant class maintains control not just through force, but by shaping cultural norms and values?
	•	A) Hegemony
	•	B) Anomie
	•	C) Pluralism
	•	D) Bureaucratization
Answer: A) Hegemony
Explanation: Cultural hegemony refers to the domination of a culturally diverse society by a ruling class who manipulate the culture—the beliefs, explanations, and values—so that their imposed worldview becomes the accepted cultural norm.

5. What is the fundamental principle behind "Responsibility to Protect" (R2P) in international law?
	•	A) States have an unconditional right to annex neighboring territories during civil conflict.
	•	B) Sovereignty is not an absolute right; the international community has a duty to intervene when a state fails to protect its population from mass atrocities.
	•	C) Developing nations must allocate a fixed percentage of GDP to global peace operations.
	•	D) International corporations must pay damages for environmental degradation in foreign jurisdictions.
Answer: B) Sovereignty is not an absolute right; the international community has a duty to intervene when a state fails to protect its population from mass atrocities.
Explanation: R2P is a global political commitment endorsed by the UN to prevent genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing, and crimes against humanity, placing human rights protection above national sovereignty when severe violations occur.

6. Which principle of international law asserts that a person cannot be returned to a country where they face torture, cruel punishment, or irreparable harm?
	•	A) Habeas corpus
	•	B) Non-refoulement
	•	C) Extradition
	•	D) Diplomatic immunity
Answer: B) Non-refoulement
Explanation: Non-refoulement is a core principle of international refugee law that prohibits states from deporting or returning asylum seekers to countries where their life or freedom would be threatened.

7. Max Weber argued that modern state authority relies heavily on which form of legitimate domination?
	•	A) Charismatic authority
	•	B) Traditional authority
	•	C) Legal-rational authority
	•	D) Feudal authority
Answer: C) Legal-rational authority
Explanation: Weber identified legal-rational authority—grounded in formalized rules, legal procedures, and bureaucratic institutions—as the defining characteristic of modern democratic governance.

8. What is "Hard Power" in contrast to "Soft Power" in foreign policy?
	•	A) Hard power relies on military force and economic coercion; soft power relies on cultural attraction and diplomacy.
	•	B) Hard power is exercised by NGOs, while soft power is exercised by national militaries.
	•	C) Hard power refers to constitutional law, while soft power refers to public opinion polls.
	•	D) Hard power is used during peace, while soft power is used strictly during wartime.
Answer: A) Hard power relies on military force and economic coercion; soft power relies on cultural attraction and diplomacy.
Explanation: Coined by Joseph Nye, hard power involves coercion through military strength or economic sanctions, while soft power persuades through cultural influence, values, and foreign relations policies.

9. What primary issue does Jürgen Habermas address with his theory of the "Public Sphere"?
	•	A) The privatization of state-owned public transport utilities.
	•	B) The ideal space in social life where individuals deliberate public matters freely and rationally to shape political action.
	•	C) The military defense of physical territorial borders.
	•	D) The statutory regulation of real estate markets in modern cities.
Answer: B) The ideal space in social life where individuals deliberate public matters freely and rationally to shape political action.
Explanation: Habermas defined the public sphere as a realm of social life where private individuals assemble, converse rationally, and form public opinion outside state or market control.

10. What is the central concern of "Cosmopolitanism" in global ethics?
	•	A) National interest should always supersede moral duties to foreign populations.
	•	B) All human beings belong to a single global moral community, regardless of national citizenship.
	•	C) Cultural practices must strictly conform to Western institutional models.
	•	D) Sovereign states should eliminate local elected assemblies in favor of city-state governance.
Answer: B) All human beings belong to a single global moral community, regardless of national citizenship.
Explanation: Cosmopolitanism posits that human beings hold moral worth as global citizens, creating moral obligations that transcend national boundaries, political borders, or local identities.

11. In neoliberal economic theory, what policy measure is prioritized to encourage economic growth?
	•	A) Expansion of state-owned monopolies and heavy price regulation
	•	B) Deregulation, privatization, and reduction of state intervention in free markets
	•	C) Total elimination of international trade and capital movement
	•	D) Nationalization of all private financial institutions
Answer: B) Deregulation, privatization, and reduction of state intervention in free markets
Explanation: Neoliberalism emphasizes free market capitalism, deregulation, privatization, and reduced government intervention as the primary drivers of global economic efficiency and development.

12. What concept describes a state in which the government lacks domestic legitimacy and cannot effectively maintain public order or basic social services?
	•	A) Totalitarian state
	•	B) Fragile or failed state
	•	C) Welfare state
	•	D) Federal state
Answer: B) Fragile or failed state
Explanation: A fragile or failed state occurs when central political authority collapses, failing to maintain security, law enforcement, or essential services within its territorial borders.

13. Which judicial institution was established by the Rome Statute in 1998 to prosecute individuals for genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity?
	•	A) International Court of Justice (ICJ)
	•	B) European Court of Human Rights (ECHR)
	•	C) International Criminal Court (ICC)
	•	D) Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA)
Answer: C) International Criminal Court (ICC)
Explanation: The International Criminal Court (ICC) prosecutes individual perpetrators of genocide, war crimes, aggression, and crimes against humanity, functioning independently of state courts.

14. What sociological term refers to the feeling of normlessness and loss of social direction experienced when social structures break down?
	•	A) Alienation
	•	B) Anomie
	•	C) Bureaucracy
	•	D) Social mobility
Answer: B) Anomie
Explanation: Émile Durkheim coined the term anomie to describe a state of normlessness where rapid social or economic changes erode shared social guidelines and collective moral coherence.

15. What is "Deliberative Democracy"?
	•	A) A political model where citizens vote directly on every single tax regulation without debate.
	•	B) A model of democracy emphasizing authentic public debate and reasoned discussion among citizens prior to decision-making.
	•	C) A system where military leaders make administrative decisions behind closed doors.
	•	D) Voting exclusively through automated algorithms without public interaction.
Answer: B) A model of democracy emphasizing authentic public debate and reasoned discussion among citizens prior to decision-making.
Explanation: Deliberative democracy holds that political decisions gain legitimacy through open, rational public discussion and debate rather than mere vote-counting.

16. What is the main thesis of Samuel Huntington's controversial "Clash of Civilizations" theory?
	•	A) Economic global markets will eliminate all socio-political conflict.
	•	B) Post-Cold War global conflicts will primarily occur along cultural and religious fault lines rather than national political ideologies.
	•	C) Technological advancement will unite all cultures into a single harmonized political state.
	•	D) Nuclear weapons proliferation will end regional border disputes permanently.
Answer: B) Post-Cold War global conflicts will primarily occur along cultural and religious fault lines rather than national political ideologies.
Explanation: Huntington argued that in the post-Cold War world, cultural and religious identities—rather than political or economic ideologies—would be the primary source of global conflict.

17. Which economic term describes a scenario where a single nation or state produces a good at a lower opportunity cost than another state?
	•	A) Absolute advantage
	•	B) Comparative advantage
	•	C) Protectionism
	•	D) Trade deficit
Answer: B) Comparative advantage
Explanation: David Ricardo's theory of comparative advantage demonstrates that international trade benefits states when each specializes in producing goods for which it incurs a lower opportunity cost.

18. "Intersectionality," a concept developed by Kimberlé Crenshaw, highlights how:
	•	A) Economic trade routes intersect with military installations.
	•	B) Various social categorizations (race, class, gender) overlap and create interconnected systems of discrimination or privilege.
	•	C) Digital technological networks merge with international legal frameworks.
	•	D) Sovereign states coordinate police forces to handle transnational crime.
Answer: B) Various social categorizations (race, class, gender) overlap and create interconnected systems of discrimination or privilege.
Explanation: Intersectionality explores how social identities (e.g., gender, race, socio-economic class) interact simultaneously to create distinct systems of disadvantage or privilege.

19. What distinguishes "Negative Rights" from "Positive Rights" in political theory?
	•	A) Negative rights require government action to provide resources; positive rights require government non-interference.
	•	B) Negative rights prevent state interference (e.g., freedom of speech); positive rights require the state to provide benefits (e.g., healthcare, education).
	•	C) Negative rights apply only during wartime; positive rights apply during peace.
	•	D) Negative rights are granted by global corporations; positive rights are granted by local courts.
Answer: B) Negative rights prevent state interference (e.g., freedom of speech); positive rights require the state to provide benefits (e.g., healthcare, education).
Explanation: Negative rights obligate state inaction (freedom from state overreach like censorship), whereas positive rights require positive state provision (delivering services like healthcare, education, or welfare).

20. What is "Cultural Relativism" in social anthropology and ethics?
	•	A) The belief that one's own culture is superior to all other cultures.
	•	B) The principle that an individual's beliefs and activities should be understood within the context of their own culture, rather than judged by external standards.
	•	C) The complete adoption of foreign cultural traditions over native customs.
	•	D) The enforcement of global legal norms upon rural populations.
Answer: B) The principle that an individual's beliefs and activities should be understood within the context of their own culture, rather than judged by external standards.
Explanation: Cultural relativism argues against judging cultural practices using foreign standards, promoting empathetic understanding of cultural behaviors within their own historical context.

21. What is the "Tragedy of the Commons"?
	•	A) The collapse of public judicial courts due to lack of political funding.
	•	B) An economic problem where individuals, acting independently in self-interest, deplete a shared, limited resource, damaging the collective good.
	•	C) The loss of regional traditional languages caused by compulsory state education.
	•	D) The strategic failure of international peace treaties during global wars.
Answer: B) An economic problem where individuals, acting independently in self-interest, deplete a shared, limited resource, damaging the collective good.
Explanation: The Tragedy of the Commons illustrates how shared open-access resources (e.g., oceans, clean air, public pasture) are overused and degraded when individuals pursue short-term self-interest over sustainability.

22. What characterizes "Euroscepticism" within contemporary European politics?
	•	A) Enthusiastic advocacy for a single unified global government.
	•	B) Criticism of, or opposition to, the political integration and authority of the European Union (EU).
	•	C) Total rejection of democratic elections across European states.
	•	D) The adoption of uniform currency across all sovereign continents.
Answer: B) Criticism of, or opposition to, the political integration and authority of the European Union (EU).
Explanation: Euroscepticism represents political opposition or skepticism regarding European integration, questioning EU supranational authority and advocating for national sovereignty.

23. In political sociology, what is "Democratic Backsliding"?
	•	A) The gradual decline of democratic quality and institutions driven by state authority or elected leaders.
	•	B) The peaceful transition from a single-party state to a multi-party representative democracy.
	•	C) The legal requirement for citizens to lower the mandatory voting age.
	•	D) The constitutional amendment process used to create local municipal boards.
Answer: A) The gradual decline of democratic quality and institutions driven by state authority or elected leaders.
Explanation: Democratic backsliding involves the state-led erosion of democratic norms, electoral integrity, free press, judicial independence, and civil liberties within existing democracies.

24. What primary role do "Transnational Advocacy Networks" (TANs) play in global governance?
	•	A) They deploy armed military forces to secure national borders.
	•	B) They link actors across borders to generate media visibility, influence global policy, and pressure state governments on specific causes.
	•	C) They control national bank interest rates and print global currencies.
	•	D) They pass legally binding international tax statutes across UN member nations.
Answer: B) They link actors across borders to generate media visibility, influence global policy, and pressure state governments on specific causes.
Explanation: TANs (comprising NGOs, activists, and international organizations) use information and persuasion strategies (the "boomerang model") to mobilize public support and hold sovereign governments accountable.

25. How does the political concept of "Social Contract Theory" explain the legitimacy of state governance?
	•	A) Authority is granted directly by divine command to royal monarchs regardless of citizen consent.
	•	B) Political authority is based on an implicit agreement where individuals surrender some natural freedoms in exchange for state protection of their remaining rights.
	•	C) States derive power through continuous military conquest over vulnerable populations.
	•	D) Government authority is established purely through commercial contract agreements between private corporations.
Answer: B) Political authority is based on an implicit agreement where individuals surrender some natural freedoms in exchange for state protection of their remaining rights.
Explanation: Advanced by thinkers like Thomas Hobbes, John Locke, and Jean-Jacques Rousseau, Social Contract Theory asserts that state legitimacy originates from the consent of the governed, who trade total unrestricted freedom for collective order and rights protection.
